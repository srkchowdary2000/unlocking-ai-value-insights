# Unlocking AI Value: Insights from the Top — Series Overview

> **What this is:** an overview of the Infosys Knowledge Institute video series *Unlocking AI Value: Insights from the Top*. It has 15 interviews, about 3 hours in total. Jeff Kavanaugh (Head, Infosys Knowledge Institute) interviewed each Infosys leader at the Infosys Connect conference in Los Angeles.
> **Source:** https://www.infosys.com/iki/videos/unlocking-ai-value-insights-top.html
> **Companion files:** `Video Notes.md` (detailed notes per video) · `transcripts\` (full transcripts) · `videos\` (MP4s)

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. **The experimentation phase is over; scale is the agenda.** The CEO's message: "The technology has already proven itself. This is the time to do these things at scale." Speed itself is now the main source of value.
2. **Productivity is the entry point, not the prize.** Copilot-style personal productivity built comfort with AI. The value now comes from reinventing whole processes and from new revenue.
3. **Models are commoditizing; context is the moat.** An enterprise's business context, data and tacit knowledge are the one input vendors cannot productize.
4. **Trust drives adoption, and guardrails make speed possible.** Security, quality engineering and governance are framed as the brakes that let you go faster.
5. **Cost now shapes the design.** "Token guzzlers," tokenomics and the ratio of input to output tokens are design constraints, not afterthoughts.
6. **This is a people transformation first.** Reskill rather than replace, redesign career architectures, and treat change management as about 80% of the work.
7. **The next customer is an AI agent.** Brands must be discoverable and persuasive to the agents shopping, comparing and transacting for consumers.
8. **Frontier models change the cyber equation.** Models can now chain low-severity vulnerabilities into critical ones, so resilience has to be measured in minutes.

## 2. Major themes

### Theme 1 — From productivity to reinvention
- Copilot is "the starting point, not the end point." Agentic AI reimagines business processes and produces insights that were impossible to generate by hand (**Shishank Gupta**).
- Many enterprises have not yet found use cases that deliver value beyond productivity (**Rafee Tarafdar**).
- Customer care should become a source of revenue (upsell, cross-sell, retention), not just a cost of service (**Venkat G**).
- Banks are reimagining every process, from onboarding to complaints and advisory (**Bal Shukla**).
- Clients now demand measurable ROI. They want process reinvention and new markets, not just tools (**Suyash Awasthi**).
- Putting agents into existing processes makes them faster. Redesigning processes around agents brings the exponential gain (**Ben Ko**).

### Theme 2 — Speed is the strategy
- Deploy with speed and scale up foundation models and platforms quickly. Work that took weeks now takes minutes (**Salil Parekh**).
- No more three-year ERP programs; acceleration is what delivers value (**Rafee Tarafdar**).
- Core banking overhauls are moving from 3–5 years to about 18 months (**Bal Shukla**).
- Working apps arrive in days or weeks, not months or years (**Suyash Awasthi**).
- A dual-speed strategy: take cost out of legacy, and build new capabilities at the speed customers expect (**Vikram Meghal**).
- On a relative basis, standing still means falling behind: "if my competitor is doing it faster, I'm losing out" (**Karmesh Vaswani**, **Bal Shukla**).

### Theme 3 — Context is the moat
- As AI vendors productize capabilities, enterprises create value by applying their *business context* to models and agents (**Rafee Tarafdar**).
- "Models will come and go… the context is your IP." Microsoft's tools hold rich user and organizational context (**Shishank Gupta**).
- A "context mesh" (a knowledge graph of the client's systems and tacit knowledge) makes AI testing more accurate and cheaper (**Venky Iyengar**).
- A client's own AI assets and context beat "some shiny new model" (**Suyash Awasthi**).
- AI can finally capture unstructured expertise that lives in people's heads, such as what nurses, technicians and operators know (**Ben Ko**).

### Theme 4 — Trust, guardrails and quality as accelerators
- "The most important thing that helps a race car go faster is the brakes." Protect the AI, and use AI for cyber (**Mayank Agarwal**).
- Trust is the biggest barrier to adoption, so quality engineering becomes the AI trust layer. Probabilistic systems need new testing disciplines (**Venky Iyengar**).
- IP, privacy and regulatory concerns ease when tools run inside the client's own environment, and legal teams feel "finally heard" (**Ben Ko**).
- Model sycophancy, hallucination and non-determinism mean humans stay in the decisions (**Suyash Awasthi**).

### Theme 5 — Token economics and cost-aware design
- Some agentic deployments were pulled back because they were too slow and costly, so cost-aware architecture is required (**Rafee Tarafdar**).
- Tokenomics is one of the enterprise challenges at scale, alongside performance, guardrails and latency (**Venkat G**).
- Keep input tokens high relative to output tokens for higher accuracy at lower cost. Build observability and a control plane for token use (**Venky Iyengar**).

### Theme 6 — People: reskill, redesign careers, manage the change
- Infosys is reskilling everyone, plans no layoffs, and hired about 20,000 graduates last year (**Salil Parekh**).
- An "ambidextrous organization," a Y-shaped career architecture and a Capability Quotient. Few companies redesign career architecture; that is the differentiator (**Shaji Mathew**).
- About 80% of the work is change management, and everyone up to the CEO must use AI personally (**Bal Shukla**).
- Mindset change and the "comfort trap" are the slowest parts; start with micro-change in a few teams (**Rafee Tarafdar**).
- Adoption needs a top-down mandate *and* bottom-up uptake, with playgrounds, sandboxes and trust (**Suyash Awasthi**, **Skyler Mattson**).
- Healthy firms redeploy staff to higher-value work, while cash-strapped firms will cut (**Venkat G**). Creative jobs get "bigger and more interesting" (**Skyler Mattson**).
- People become mentors and guides of agents rather than doers (**Shishank Gupta**).
- Asking "does a human need to do this?" creates new roles (**Vikram Meghal**).
- A learning-to-career pipeline for people outside the company (**Kate Maloney**).

### Theme 7 — Designing for AI agents as customers
- Shoppers now ask ChatGPT, so brands are moving TV budgets to influencers to earn third-party endorsements. Experiences are built "for the customer and the agent" (**Skyler Mattson**).
- The consumer of tomorrow "has a number of agents working for them," so the four Ps (product, price, promotion, place) must appeal to those agents (**Karmesh Vaswani**).
- Agentic commerce with human-in-the-loop limits: "auto-pay on steroids" (**Bal Shukla**).

### Theme 8 — Frontier-model cyber risk
- Frontier models ("Mythos" and others) can chain low-severity vulnerabilities into critical ones, and defenders have the same capability. The response: visibility of vulnerability chains, compensating controls, and resilience in minutes (**Mayank Agarwal**).
- Travel companies hold sensitive personal data and must guard both internal systems and the perimeter (**Samik Ghatak**).
- Networks are critical national infrastructure and must isolate attacked segments automatically (**Vikram Meghal**).

## 5. Recurring obstacles
- **Data readiness and quality:** untagged calls, edge data spread across channels, "garbage in, garbage out" (Venkat, Skyler, Suyash, Samik).
- **Legacy and integration:** mainframe cores, several cores after acquisitions, "why is my bill high?" questions that span systems, connecting robots to warehouse, manufacturing and ERP systems (Bal, Venkat, Ben, Vikram).
- **Organizational debt:** technical, process, data and strategy debt, adding up to a risk of "digital bankruptcy" (Suyash).
- **Trust, IP, privacy and regulation:** IP leaks, patient data, and the FDA limiting continuously learning models (Ben, Venky, Mayank).
- **Readiness at scale:** performance, guardrails, latency and token cost (Venkat, Rafee, Venky).
- **Mindset and change:** the comfort trap, slow risk approvals, adoption theatrics (Rafee, Bal, Suyash, Skyler).
- **Too many choices:** a flood of AI-native point solutions and new models every day (Suyash).

## 6. Recommended actions for leaders
1. **Move from pilots to scale.** Pick use cases that go beyond productivity and tie each one to measurable revenue, cost or risk outcomes.
2. **Architect for change.** Abstract models, frameworks, runtimes and platforms. Map each layer's maturity and decide whether to build, buy or wait.
3. **Invest in context and data.** Build knowledge graphs or a context mesh, fix data tagging and quality, and treat context as IP.
4. **Design for cost.** Make token economics, observability and a control plane for agents part of the architecture from the start.
5. **Make trust the accelerator.** Guardrails, AI-specific quality engineering, in-environment tooling, and cyber resilience measured in minutes.
6. **Transform the people side as deliberately as the tech.** Reskill at scale, redesign career architecture, combine a top-down mandate with bottom-up experimentation, and start with micro-change in a few teams.
7. **Prepare for the agentic customer.** Make products, content and offers legible to AI agents, not just humans.
8. **Run two speeds.** Use AI to take cost out of legacy, while building new capabilities at the speed customers expect.
